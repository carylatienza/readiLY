**A. Questions to ask the mentor**

**Business model & revenue**

* Our end users are public school teachers, but our paying customers are DepEd, LGUs, and private schools. How do we validate willingness-to-pay when the user and the buyer are different people?  
* B2G sales cycles with DepEd/LGUs are notoriously long. Should our first revenue come from private schools or NGO/CSR partnerships instead, and how do we sequence that?  
* What's a realistic pricing structure for this market — per student, per teacher, per school, per division? What comparable deals have you seen in Philippine edtech?  
* We haven't built financial projections yet (no revenue targets, cost estimates, or break-even analysis in the plan). What level of financial detail does a pre-product startup at our stage actually need?  
* How should we think about funding — grants (DOST, DTI, Startup QC), competitions, or angel investment — given we're pre-product?

**Validation & pilot**

* We're planning a pilot with 1–3 schools, 2–3 teachers, 100–150 students over 2–4 weeks. Is that enough to produce credible evidence, and what specific metrics would convince a DepEd division or an investor?  
* What's the right way to get pilot access to public schools — do we need a DepEd research permit or division-level MOA, and how long does that take?  
* How many teacher interviews or classroom observations should we do before building anything, and what should we be trying to disprove?

**Competition & positioning**

* DepEd already mandates Phil-IRI and CRLA. Should we position as a digital layer on top of those official assessments (with score mapping), or as a separate tool? Which is easier to sell?  
* Global tools like Amira Learning and Microsoft Reading Progress already do AI oral reading assessment. Is "localized for Filipino/Tagalog/mother-tongue + offline-first" a durable moat, or do we need something more?

**Regulatory & risk**

* What are the compliance requirements for processing children's voice data in the Philippines (Data Privacy Act / NPC rules), and at what stage do we need a DPO or privacy review?  
* What are the biggest reasons you've seen edtech pilots in Philippine public schools fail to convert into paid contracts?

**Team & execution**

* We're a two-person student team. What key gaps (ML engineering, education domain expert, B2G sales) should we fill first, and how — hires, advisors, or partnerships?  
* Given limited resources, what should our MVP scope be — full diagnostic AI, or a simpler version (e.g., timed WPM + accuracy on-device) that we can pilot fast?

Any other questions or suggestions will be highly appreciated. Thank you ^^

**B. Mentor's Comments & Suggestions (Cody Flores)**

**Business model & revenue**
* **On validating willingness-to-pay when the user and buyer are different:** "Value for buyers have to be clear for them to have willingness to buy. Otherwise, find out what are adjacent education-related programs that these LGUs or nat'l govt units already have in place that have a budget or an ordinance and see what this solution can supplement or improve. For example, if the gov't / LGU already has programs in relation to diagnosis, testing, development, or initiatives for this etc., doon niyo hahanapan ng budget. For private schools naman, there needs to be direct value for them as well."
* **On sequencing revenue from private schools or NGO/CSRs:** "Private schools would be a lot more promising unless you already have inroads to CSR-based partnerships. Sales cycles for private are a lot more standardized, but efforts to cultivate and build relationships with LGUs must be done adjacently. But I recommend that the revenue stream from private is just as sustainable and scalable as it's really difficult to rely on public sector for your business model"
* **On pricing structure and comparable deals:** "Pricing structure will always depend on who will find most value out of this. Willingness to pay also increases if the product market fit is strong. Focus on that and you'll know the direction of your GTM all the way to your pricing. My recommendation is per student because our deped already struggles to fund our schools and our teachers. What would make your solution change the status quo? If this value prop is clear to them, then yes, better for scaling ang per school / per division structure."
* **On the level of financial detail needed for a pre-product startup:** "Refer to EJ's talk -- but for me, I'd say it's good to know what your costs will be, and a rough idea of your pricing + how big the target market is. Estimations are fine as we know this is theoretical."
* **On funding strategies given the pre-product stage:** "Funding is still possible at this stage as long as you have gone through compelling product-market validation exercises. Raising from friends and family for MVP testing and piloting is extremely common, and will build your case."

**Validation & pilot**
* **On whether a 1–3 school pilot size is enough:** "From an outsider's perspective, I'd think it was enough. This is a typical pilot size for your stage of a startup. But in terms of metrics that convince deped, kailangan niyo talaga mabangga sa mga existing initiatives nila, development roadmaps, and goals. Only then would your findings be compelling for them."
* **On getting pilot access to public schools:** "I'm not too familiar with this process. Probably better to do pilot tests from private schools first, and as you build your network in this space, they will advise you on the best practices to pilot in public schools as well."
* **On what to disprove before building anything:** "These are things that you can only really find out once you've committed and immersed yourselves into the process. I'd suggest doing 2-3 tests per potential market segment and see where the pain points persist the most."

**Competition & positioning**
* **On positioning as a digital layer on top of Phil-IRI and CRLA:** "Oh definitely! This is exactly what I'm pertaining to when I mentioned existing programs that have existing budget. It's a really good lead. I'd think it was a good idea to position as a digital layer because it makes the validation and product development and sales a lot more direct and curated and targeted. That's a solid POC opportunity if it works well. Successful POCs converts to a sale and it will make them feel dependent on your tech solution."
* **On having a "localized + offline-first" durable moat:** "Find out why these existing solutions are unable to localize and be available offline. If it's something that only you can bypass and address, then it's definitely a moat. If not, it buys you enough time, but make sure that time is used to have your userbase extremely loyal that it would cost them so much to switch."

**Regulatory & risk**
* **On Data Privacy Act / NPC compliance requirements:** "I'm not so familiar with this, so best to have a separate consultant for regulations :) Apologies!"
* **On why edtech pilots in public schools fail to convert:** "Super pain to work with the government. It's not really sustainable unless you have a solid champion who can vouch for you and speak for you huhuhu It's a lot of deep relationship building, and buy in from the right people. There's no formula to this unfortunately."

**Team & execution**
* **On key gaps to fill first (ML vs domain expert vs sales):** "I'd say it's really the education domain expert / B2G sales expert mainly because the tech development will naturally follow once you've invested into understanding the market, the pain points, and the product-market validation."
* **On MVP scope given limited resources:** "this is something you can only determine once you've completed your more in-depth market study. I'd say piloting fast through a simpler MVP might be your better bet if you have that first inroad/touch point to your market because you can always build once you have a better understanding on what the market actually demands / what pain points are they actually experiencing. The purpose of that Pilot, more often than not, is really on building trust."
