# **An Exhaustive Analysis of the Startup QC Program: Policy Objectives, Selection Mechanisms, and Ecosystem Impact**

The intersection of municipal governance and technological innovation has historically been fraught with regulatory friction and a lack of risk capital. Local Government Units (LGUs) in emerging economies traditionally limit their economic mandates to basic infrastructure provision, regulatory compliance, and business permit administration. However, recognizing the limitations of traditional economic development frameworks, the Quezon City Government instituted a radical policy departure to actively cultivate a technology and innovation ecosystem. Established under City Ordinance SP-3109, S-2022, and formally operationalized in October 2022, the Startup QC Program is recognized as the first and largest LGU-led startup incubation initiative in the Philippines.  
This comprehensive report examines the strategic architecture of the Startup QC initiative. It analyzes the fundamental policy objectives underpinning the program, dissects the rigorous evaluation and selection mechanisms utilized to identify high-potential ventures, details the multi-phased incubation processes, and evaluates the empirical outcomes of its early cohorts. Furthermore, the analysis generates deeper insights into how municipal interventions can effectively stimulate grassroots technological advancement, bridge the early-stage funding gap, and align private entrepreneurial ambition with pressing civic and socio-economic challenges.

## **The Strategic Purpose and Legislative Foundation**

The conceptualization of Startup QC must be understood within the broader macroeconomic imperatives of the Philippine digital economy. The program was designed to fulfill four primary strategic mandates: to create new employment opportunities, to stimulate the entrepreneurial spirit at the grassroots level, to promote technological and civic innovation, and to strengthen a self-sustaining startup culture within Quezon City.

### **Aligning Innovation with Municipal and National Agendas**

The initiative is deeply anchored in a broader legislative and executive vision. It serves as a direct operationalization of the local administration's 14-point agenda, which prioritizes making Quezon City the preferred destination for high-growth businesses and establishing the locale as the preeminent "Startup Capital of the Philippines". Furthermore, it localizes the national mandates set forth by Republic Act No. 11337, otherwise known as the "Innovative Startup Act." This national legislation declares it the policy of the State to foster inclusive growth through an innovative economy, streamlining government and non-government initiatives to create new jobs, improve production, and advance innovation.  
The purpose of Startup QC extends far beyond basic economic stimulation; it is fundamentally designed as a mechanism for civic problem-solving. The LGU actively seeks to empower its citizens to build innovative business models that provide tangible solutions to deeply entrenched social issues and urban dilemmas. By focusing investments on sectors such as sustainability, health technology, educational technology, agricultural technology, and governance technology, the city is effectively utilizing startup incubation as an outsourced research and development apparatus to address complex urban challenges.

### **The Evolution of the LGU into a Venture Catalyst**

A critical underlying insight derived from the creation of Startup QC is the transformation of the LGU’s role in the economy. By offering equity-free grants of up to Php 1,000,000, the municipal government is willingly absorbing the highest risk phase of venture building—the precarious transition from ideation to a commercialized Minimum Viable Product (MVP). Unlike private venture capitalists who require equity dilution in exchange for early-stage capital, the LGU's return on investment is measured strictly in positive macroeconomic externalities: specialized job creation, the eventual expansion of the municipal tax base, and the deployment of technologies that elevate the quality of life for its constituents.  
This non-dilutive, equity-free model acts as a powerful market signal. When a startup successfully graduates from the rigorous phases of Startup QC, it has been vetted, de-risked, and validated by a government entity. This governmental endorsement makes the venture highly attractive to downstream private investors, venture capital firms, and institutional accelerators, effectively bridging the "valley of death" that typically claims early-stage startups in emerging markets.

### **Inter-Agency Collaboration and the Triple-Helix Model**

The strategic purpose of Startup QC relies heavily on the "Triple-Helix" model of innovation, which posits that sustained economic growth is driven by the dynamic interactions between government, academia, and private industry. The Quezon City LGU actively co-opts national agencies to align local initiatives with national digital infrastructure goals. A prominent example is the Memorandum of Understanding signed between the Quezon City Government and the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI). This partnership facilitates knowledge-sharing, policy support, and the promotion of the initiative on a national scale, culminating in the establishment of the Startup Business One-Stop Shop (SBOSS), with Quezon City acting as the pilot site for streamlined startup registration and support.

## **Architectural Framework: Program Tracks and Incentive Structures**

To accurately address the varying maturity levels and capital requirements of local innovators, the Startup QC architecture is bifurcated into two distinct tracks: the Professional Category and the Student Category.

### **The Professional Category**

The Professional Category targets early-stage startups and established entrepreneurs who already possess a Minimum Viable Product and are seeking the capital and mentorship necessary to scale their operations. Under the governing Ordinance SP-3109, S-2022, a qualifying startup business is defined as any person or entity in the Philippines aiming to refine an innovative product, process, or business model that is both scalable and replicable within Quezon City.  
The program recognizes three specific domains of innovation for professional applicants:

* **Innovative Product:** A good or service that is entirely new or significantly improved regarding technical specifications, component materials, software architecture, user-friendliness, or other functional characteristics.  
* **Innovative Process:** A new or significantly improved production or delivery method achieved through changes in techniques, equipment, or software.  
* **Innovative Business Model:** A new organizational method applied to business practices, workplace organization, or external corporate relations.

Participants successfully navigating the professional category are eligible to receive a maximum financial grant of up to Php 1,000,000 per beneficiary. The final disbursed amount is not a flat rate; rather, it is highly customized and calculated according to the venture's specific business plan, the category of the applicant, and an approved list of operational supplies and expenses heavily scrutinized by the Startup QC Program Committee.

### **The Student Competition Category (Squads)**

Recognizing that the foundation of any sustainable innovation ecosystem begins in academia, the Startup QC Student Competition aims to foster a culture of entrepreneurship among the youth. Operating as a highly structured business plan and pitching competition organized in seasonal cohorts referred to as "Squads," the program targets students from various schools, colleges, and universities within the Philippines. Students are actively encouraged to develop solutions addressing key civic sectors, notably Agriculture, Finance, Education, Health, Information Technology, and Culture and Arts.  
Unlike the professional track, which focuses concentrated capital on a smaller number of mature startups, the student category utilizes a tiered prize architecture. This model distributes micro-grants across multiple promising teams to maximize the volume of prototypes funded at the academic level.

| **Grand Champion** | 100,000 | Restricted to one team per category |
| **1st Runner Up** | 75,000 | Restricted to one team per category |
| **2nd Runner Up** | 50,000 | Restricted to one team per category |

The strategic implication of this micro-grant structure is highly profound. An injection of Php 15,000 to Php 35,000 is frequently sufficient for a university engineering or business team to transition a theoretical concept into a functional hardware prototype or software wireframe. By seeding these concepts early, the student competition acts as a direct talent feeder system for the professional track in subsequent years, ensuring a continuous pipeline of homegrown innovation.

## **The Mechanics of Selection: How Startup QC Identifies Winners**

The process of selecting grant recipients in the Startup QC program is characterized by stringent eligibility requirements, demanding application deliverables, and a multi-disciplinary evaluation matrix. The program leverages municipal bureaucratic oversight to ensure fiduciary responsibility while integrating private-sector frameworks to assess commercial viability.

### **Foundational Eligibility Criteria**

To ensure that public funds are utilized responsibly and align with the geographic and economic mandates of the local government, the program enforces strict baseline eligibility rules for both tracks.

| Evaluation Criterion | Professional Category Requirements | Student Category Requirements |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Age Threshold** | Must be at least 21 years of age | Must be at least 18 years of age |
| **Geographic Nexus** | At least one (1) member must be a resident of Quezon City | At least one (1) member must be a resident of Quezon City |
| **Legal Standing** | No prior convictions (labor, civil, administrative, or criminal) | No prior convictions (labor, civil, administrative, or criminal) |
| **Conflict of Interest** | No relatives on the screening committee up to the 4th degree of consanguinity | No relatives on the screening committee up to the 4th degree of consanguinity |
| **Development Stage** | Must demonstrate a functional Minimum Viable Product (MVP) | Must be enrolled in a PH educational institution; viable business plan or proof of concept required |
| **Originality** | Proposed project must not duplicate any ongoing initiatives of the Quezon City Government | Proposed project must not duplicate any ongoing initiatives of the Quezon City Government |

Table 2: Comparative Eligibility Requirements for Startup QC Applicant Tracks.  
The geographic requirement—specifically stipulating that only one team member must reside in Quezon City—reveals a highly nuanced and strategic policy insight. Rather than demanding that the entire corporate entity be domiciled in the city or that all founders be local residents, this flexible rule allows Quezon City-based founders to recruit top-tier technical, operational, and business talent from across the entire Philippine archipelago, or even internationally, while still qualifying for municipal support. This policy actively prevents geographic insularity and promotes a cosmopolitan flow of intellectual capital into the local ecosystem.

### **The Application Artifacts and E-Services Integration**

Reflecting the city's broader commitment to digital transformation and the ease of doing business, the entire application intake process is digitized through the QC E-services portal. Applicants to both categories are required to submit core artifacts that mirror standard private venture capital application procedures, enforcing discipline and clarity upon the founders.  
The application demands two primary deliverables:

1. **The Video Pitch:** A maximum five-minute video presentation where applicants must clearly articulate their business plan, demonstrate their Minimum Viable Product, and validate their market assumptions.  
2. **The Comprehensive Business Proposal:** A strictly formatted, 10-page (or 10-slide) document that forces founders to distill complex ventures into standardized strategic components.

The mandated structure of the business proposal ensures that the LGU's evaluation committee can conduct standardized, apples-to-apples comparisons across wildly disparate industries (e.g., comparing an agricultural hardware startup to a financial technology software platform). The proposal must systematically address the following ten criteria:

| Proposal Section | Analytical Requirement |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Company Name** | The official nomenclature of the startup or student team. |
| **Executive Summary** | A concise, high-level overview synthesizing the entire business plan. |
| **Why Join Startup QC?** | A statement outlining the founder's motivation and alignment with municipal socio-economic goals. |
| **Problem** | A clear definition of the specific issue the business addresses and the market gap it intends to fill. |
| **Goals & Objectives** | Detailed outcomes the team aims to achieve, alongside specific metrics to measure success over time. |
| **Solution** | The primary operational approach the business concept will take to solve the defined problem and deliver unique value. |
| **Feasibility** | A rigorous breakdown of the technical feasibility of the product and the operational feasibility of the business plan. |
| **Market Analysis** | A thorough presentation analyzing current market trends, target demographics, and the competitive landscape. |
| **Business Model** | A detailed explanation of the mechanics by which the company intends to create value and generate sustainable revenue. |
| **Team Profile** | A brief introduction to the human capital driving the venture, highlighting the founders' notable achievements and domain expertise. |

Table 3: Mandated Ten-Point Structure for Startup QC Business Proposals.  
The requirement for a detailed Business Model and Feasibility analysis is particularly telling. It demonstrates that the local government is not merely dispensing grants for theoretical research; rather, it demands that subsidized ventures possess a realistic path to financial independence. A startup that cannot generate its own revenue will eventually become a continuous drain on public subsidies, which contradicts the core objective of building a self-sustaining ecosystem.

### **The Evaluation Matrix: Five Pillars of Success**

The process of evaluating these comprehensive applications represents a departure from traditional civic procurement. Traditional government procurement heavily prioritizes the lowest responsible bidder for a highly defined set of goods or services. In contrast, Startup QC adopts a venture-growth evaluation matrix, where entries are judged by a panel of experts across five core criteria designed to quantify future potential:

1. **Innovativeness and Creativity:** This metric assesses whether the product introduces a genuinely novel technology or applies an existing technology to a previously underserved market in a creative manner.  
2. **Sustainability and Viability:** The evaluators assess whether the business model can survive beyond the lifecycle of the LGU's financial grant. It requires a realistic path to profitability, positive unit economics, and operational resilience.  
3. **Relevance:** The proposed solution must address an acute, universally recognized problem within the current socio-economic landscape, proving that market demand exists.  
4. **Impact:** This criterion quantifies the scale of the societal benefit. Evaluators look for ventures that promote economic inclusivity, environmental sustainability, public health, or educational access.  
5. **Team Strength:** Acknowledging the venture capital maxim that execution is more critical than ideation, this metric assesses whether the founding team possesses the requisite domain expertise, technical skills, and psychological resilience to execute their proposed business plan.

### **The Startup QC Program Committee: Institutional Oversight**

The final authority on selecting the winning startups and authorizing the disbursement of municipal funds lies exclusively with the Startup QC Program Committee. The composition of this committee is a masterclass in cross-departmental civic governance, ensuring that multiple facets of public administration—from economic promotion to legislative oversight and academic integration—are represented in the investment decision.  
According to the provisions of Ordinance SP-3109, the committee is composed of representatives from the following offices:

* Office of the City Mayor (OCM) \- Acting as the Program Sponsor  
* Office of the City Administrator (OCA) \- Acting as the Committee Chairman  
* Local Economic Development and Investment Promotions Office (LEDIPO)  
* Small Business Cooperatives Development and Promotions Office (SBCDPO)  
* Investment Affairs Office (IAO)  
* Education Affairs Office (EAO)  
* Quezon City Youth Development Office (QCYDO)  
* Quezon City University (QCU)  
* Quezon City Council

The inclusion of specific departments allows for highly specialized evaluations. For instance, the Education Affairs Office can accurately evaluate the feasibility of edtech startups, while the Youth Development Office ensures that the student category aligns with broader youth empowerment goals.  
This multi-sectoral committee is endowed with vast administrative powers. It is responsible for reviewing baseline eligibility, conducting the final evaluation of applications, determining the exact, customized grant amount to be awarded to selected applicants, and auditing the overarching implementation of the program. Furthermore, to ensure rigorous fiduciary oversight over taxpayer funds, the committee holds the authority to disqualify applicants at any stage, conduct unannounced on-the-spot visits to monitor business progress, and require the submission of detailed receipts and invoices prior to and following the issuance of the financial awards.

## **Operationalizing Innovation: The Three-Phase Incubation Journey**

Securing selection into the Startup QC cohort is merely the beginning of the program. The initiative is structured not just as a funding mechanism, but as a rigorous, multi-month incubation journey designed to transform raw concepts into highly scalable enterprises. Recognizing that municipal bureaucrats are not inherently equipped to provide highly specialized venture acceleration, the LGU wisely partners with third-party "accelerator partners" or co-organizers—such as Launchgarage and StartUp Village—to manage the pedagogical, strategic, and logistical aspects of the incubation phases.  
The incubation journey is divided into three highly curated phases, mandated by the program's Terms of Reference.

### **Phase 1: Application, Evaluation, and Sourcing**

The initial phase revolves around aggressive sourcing, filtering, and ecosystem mapping. The contracted accelerator is mandated to assist the LGU in sourcing at least 30 highly qualified professional startups and a minimum of 50 student teams per cohort. The accelerator partner actively coordinates with the LGU to conduct the preliminary evaluation and shortlisting of these entries. For the student category, the sourcing strategy is highly localized; the program establishes school-based "Startup QC ambassadors" and defines their roles to facilitate grassroots talent identification directly from university campuses and student organizations.

### **Phase 2: Intensive Business Development and Mentorship**

Once the final cohort is established (typically accommodating a minimum of five professional startups per batch), the intensive Business Development phase commences. The primary objective of this critical phase is to provide entrepreneurs with the specialized assistance required to refine their business ideas, pressure-test their assumptions, and solidify their company's business model from the ground up.  
This transformation is achieved through customized LEAD (Learning, Engagement, and Development) Sessions. The contracted accelerator is strictly required by the Terms of Reference to provide comprehensive mentorship management. This includes onboarding a minimum of five dedicated coaches and maintaining an active pool of at least 20 seasoned professional mentors. These mentors must possess extensive, real-world experience (minimum of one year working in or with a startup) across specialized fields such as Intellectual Property, Venture Capital, Business Development, Legal Compliance, Finance, Modern Technology, Agriculture, and the Internet of Things (IoT).  
The mandatory mentorship curriculum is exhaustive and mirrors the syllabus of elite global accelerators. It must cover the following topics in depth:

* **Startup Fundamentals and Planning:** Lean Startup Canvas methodologies, Value Proposition design, comprehensive Market Analysis, Go-To-Market Strategies, Customer Segmentation, Operational Frameworks, Unit Economics, and Relevant Startup Metrics.  
* **Product and Technology:** Product Development lifecycles, Product Roadmapping, achieving Product-Market Fit, and highly technical Technology Stack Consultations.  
* **Finance, Valuation, and Corporate Governance:** Investment strategies, Fundraising and Financing Options, Startup Valuation methodologies, managing a Capitalization Table, negotiating Startup Term Sheets, formal Accounting, Incorporation and Corporate Structuring, Back-Office Processes, and Legal, Risk, and Compliance management.  
* **Marketing and Human Resources:** Online Marketing Strategy Planning, Founder Backgrounds and Competency Building, HR and People Management, Employee Retention and Vesting schedules, and the protection of Intellectual Property.  
* **Ecosystem Awareness:** Navigating the Philippine Tech Ecosystem and understanding Global Tech Trends.

For student innovators, this phase also heavily involves integrating participation from university-based Technology Business Incubators (TBIs), ensuring a seamless and supported transition between academic research projects and commercial market applications.

### **Phase 3: Product Development, Acceleration, and Demo Day**

The final phase focuses entirely on tangible execution and market deployment. For companies that enter the program already possessing an MVP, this phase accelerates the growth of their business through intensive resource allocation, advanced education, and strategic market linkages.  
Crucially, the actual disbursement of the Php 1,000,000 financial grant is not provided as an upfront lump sum; it is entirely milestone-based. During this phase, the program committee, in conjunction with the accelerator partner, determines highly specific Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) that serve as the basis for startup growth evaluation and subsequent grant awarding. Startups are continuously guided and stringently monitored as they execute against these customized KPIs.  
The culmination of the entire programmatic journey is the **Demo Day** (or Pitch Day). During this high-stakes event, the founders are required to pitch their refined business models and demonstrate their products live to a curated, high-level audience composed of LGU officials, private venture capitalists, institutional partners, and broader ecosystem stakeholders. It is at this juncture that the final winners are formally recognized, and the equity-free grants are officially awarded. The Demo Day serves a dual strategic purpose: it acts as the final evaluation and public accountability checkpoint for the LGU's investment, while simultaneously serving as a high-visibility platform for the startups to secure follow-on, equity-based funding from private investors present in the audience.

## **Ecosystem Partnerships: The Foundation of Sustainable Acceleration**

An analysis of Startup QC reveals that the local government is acutely aware of its own limitations regarding deep technological expertise and venture capital network access. To bridge this gap, the LGU relies on an extensive network of ecosystem partnerships.  
The program is heavily supported by the Philippines' premier academic institutions, including the University of the Philippines (UP) Diliman, Ateneo de Manila University (ADMU), Miriam College, the Technological Institute of the Philippines (TIP), and the Thames International Business School. By embedding the program within these academic hubs, Startup QC ensures a continuous influx of cutting-edge academic research and highly skilled STEM talent.  
Simultaneously, industry partners and established startup enablers, such as StartUp Village and Launchgarage, provide the crucial private-sector pragmatism needed to ensure these ventures are commercially viable and investable. This collaborative approach ensures that the startups receive holistic support, balancing civic responsibility with aggressive commercial scaling strategies.

## **Cultivating Future Talent: The Impact of Student Squads**

While the professional track focuses on market-ready commercialization, the Startup QC Student Competition (organized into sequential "Squads") serves as a critical upstream policy intervention. Recognizing that university students often possess high-level technical engineering skills but lack basic business acumen and risk capital, the LGU utilizes these competitions to convert purely academic projects into viable commercial ventures.

### **Squad 1 (2024): Revolutionizing Rural Healthcare and Supply Chains**

In the inaugural iteration of the student competition in 2024, the program witnessed immense enthusiasm. Out of 82 applications, 39 finalists were shortlisted, resulting in 27 teams receiving varying cash awards totaling Php 725,000.  
The Grand Champion, winning the top prize of Php 100,000, was **Rise Rural Philippines**. This team exemplified the collaborative nature of the ecosystem, featuring a cross-university roster of students from UP Los Baños, Pamantasan ng Lungsod ng Maynila, and Ateneo de Manila University. They developed the **Araw-Kalinga Box (AKB)**, a highly innovative "smart backpack" system designed specifically for maternal healthcare workers operating in Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Areas (GIDA). The AKB integrates a solar-powered ultrasound machine, a cold box for vaccine storage, and a sterile compartment for medical instruments, representing a massive leap in portable healthcare infrastructure.  
The second-place prize (Php 75,000) was awarded to **ESEA** (from ADMU), an application designed to optimize the highly fragmented fisheries supply chain by connecting fisherfolk directly with end consumers. The third-place prize (Php 50,000) went to **NYHA Robotics** (from UP). Crucially, NYHA Robotics utilized this initial student capital to refine their product, eventually successfully transitioning into the Professional Cohort 3, perfectly demonstrating the intended pipeline effect of the student competition.

### **Squad 2 (2025): Urban Infrastructure, Climate Resilience, and Agritech**

The subsequent year, Squad 2 saw a significant increase in participation, with 121 applicants narrowed down to 37 finalists. A total prize pool of Php 735,000 was distributed among 30 student awardees.  
The Php 100,000 Grand Prize was awarded to **Plastipave**, developed by a multidisciplinary team of students from the Ateneo de Manila University's John Gokongwei School of Management (JGSOM). Plastipave represents a brilliant dual-solution addressing two critical urban problems: plastic pollution and urban flooding. The student engineers developed a sustainable paving block that securely entombs waste plastic within cement. Tested in independent laboratories, the blocks exceed the 3,000 psi strength requirement necessary for public bike lanes and sidewalks, and the product has already moved into active pilot testing within various sites in Quezon City.  
Other notable winners in Squad 2 included:

* **Banakeeps Innovators** (STI College–Novaliches): Won the Php 75,000 second prize for developing sustainable, plant-based menstrual products, offering an eco-friendly alternative for women's reproductive health.  
* **SwineScan** (Far Eastern University – Institute of Technology): Won the Php 50,000 third prize for engineering a system that utilizes artificial intelligence to monitor livestock health, enabling smarter and more efficient agricultural management.  
* **Bukonstruct** (ADMU): A silver-award winning project that engineered flat-packable school desk chairs using medium-density fibreboard (MDF) made from coconut fiber. This innovation significantly reduces the logistical and transportation costs of delivering school desks to remote rural areas by up to 80%.

The outcomes of the student competitions highlight the profound socio-economic impact of university-level research when paired with early municipal acceleration. Projects like Plastipave and the Araw-Kalinga Box require hardware research and development, which is notoriously difficult to fund at the undergraduate level without diluting ownership or taking on predatory debt. The local government's micro-grants serve as essential catalytic capital, moving these projects out of the sterile academic laboratory and into the physical environment where they can effect real change.

## **Rigorous Monitoring, Evaluation, and Fiduciary Accountability**

The disbursement of up to Php 1,000,000 per professional startup and the management of millions of pesos in total program funds are heavily scrutinized by the LGU. To maintain strict fiduciary responsibility over public taxpayer funds, the Startup QC program has established rigorous monitoring and evaluation (M\&E) frameworks that rival institutional venture capital diligence processes.  
The operational Terms of Reference for the accelerator partner mandate a strict, quarterly evaluation matrix known as "Venture Acceleration Measurement." This framework guarantees continuous oversight throughout the lifecycle of the cohort:

* **Quarter 1 (Framework Development):** The accelerator must define and develop the methodological framework, establish KPIs directly aligned with the overarching program objectives, map inputs, and design robust data collection tools such as surveys, interview guides, and reporting templates.  
* **Quarter 2 (Data Gathering):** The program executes the gathering of structured quantitative and qualitative data from all program beneficiaries. This data tracks business performance, specific innovation outputs, and tangible socio-economic contributions generated by the startups.  
* **Quarter 3 & 4 (Analysis and Insights Generation):** The gathered data is consolidated to conduct comparative analyses. The accelerator drafts comprehensive Monitoring & Evaluation Reports, generates actionable policy recommendations for the LGU, submits interim briefings, and presents the final results to the committee.

Furthermore, the bureaucratic structure of the Startup QC Program Committee empowers it to conduct internal audits and unannounced, on-the-spot visits to the operating premises of the startups. This ensures that the approved list of operational supplies and expenses are strictly adhered to, and all expenditures are backed by verifiable receipts and invoices. This meticulous approach guarantees that the program remains a highly accountable engine of economic development, shielding the LGU from the risks of speculative deployment of civic funds.

## **Expanding the Horizon: Legislative Evolution and Institutionalization**

To ensure that the immense momentum generated by Startup QC is not lost to changing political administrations or shifting local priorities, the Quezon City government has taken aggressive steps to permanently institutionalize the startup ecosystem within its long-term legislative framework.  
While Ordinance SP-3109 established the incubation program and its initial funding mechanism, the city recognized that one-time grants are insufficient to sustain a mature technology hub. Consequently, the LGU is advancing a more comprehensive, structural economic policy: the **Business Investment and Trade Incentives for Startups (BITIS) Ordinance**.  
The proposed BITIS Ordinance is designed to bridge incentives toward innovative startups, moving far beyond initial equity-free grants to offer sustained, long-term fiscal support and tax relief. Driven by the Local Economic Development and Investment Promotions Office under the broader "Invest with QC" campaign, the ordinance aims to create a highly supportive regulatory and fiscal environment that encourages mature entrepreneurs to scale their businesses and headquarter them permanently within the city.  
The amendment of existing municipal incentive codes (specifically, the QC Economic Development Incentives Ordinance of 2024, Ordinance No. SP-3296, S-2024) seeks to rectify outdated terminologies, clarify legal interpretations, and perfectly align municipal tax incentives with the overarching goal of attracting high-growth tech investments.  
By formulating the BITIS ordinance, Quezon City is essentially mimicking the aggressive economic development and tax-incentive strategies typically employed by sovereign nations (such as Singapore or Estonia) but applying them at a municipal level. The explicit legislative goal of amending the Startup QC ordinance to "attract startups from other areas" suggests that Quezon City is actively engaging in a domestic war for high-level technological talent. By diversifying future funding sources beyond the LGU's initial equity-free grants to include structured pathways for private sector venture capital investments and national government agency funding, the local government is successfully transitioning from being merely an incubator to acting as the overarching manager of a highly complex, self-sustaining innovation ecosystem.
